don't get upsold

you came in for one thing. leave with one thing.

Picture going in for a $15 charger and coming out with a case, a screen protector and two years of “protection”. Nobody planned that. It happened one small yes at a time.

Don't do this

  1. Jordan hey! what can I get you?
  2. Sam just a USB-C charger pls
  3. Jordan the fast one's only $10 more
  4. Sam oh ok sure
  5. Jordan want a 2-year protection plan on that?
  6. Sam …I guess?
  7. Jordan most people grab the cable bundle too. and if you join Rewards+ today you save 10%!
  8. Sam wait, how is it $96
USB-C charger$15.00
Fast upgrade$10.00
2-year protection$12.99
Cable bundle$24.99
Rewards+, yearly$39.00
Member discount−$6.30
Total$95.68

Jordan never lied. Every add-on was a fair offer on its own, and each one only cost “a little more”. Nobody agrees to a $96 charger. Almost anyone agrees to five small yeses in a row.

Upsells work because no feels rude, a quick yes feels efficient, and whatever you just agreed to makes the next thing look small. None of that says whether you need it.

The same goes for:

  • “It's only $3 more for the large.”
  • “Do you want to protect that?”
  • “Most people go for the bundle.”
  • “This deal ends today.”
  • “Sign up and save 10% on this order.”
  • “Start your free trial, cancel anytime.”
  • A “priority shipping” box, already ticked.

Just buy the thing you came for.

Do this instead

  1. Jordan hey! what can I get you?
  2. Sam just a USB-C charger pls
  3. Jordan the fast one's only $10 more
  4. Sam just the regular one, thanks!
  5. Jordan protection plan?
  6. Sam no thanks
  7. Jordan sure, that's $15
USB-C charger$15.00
Total$15.00

You don't owe anyone a reason. “No thanks” is a whole sentence, and the person asking has heard it a hundred times today. They aren't offended. They're reading from a script.

If a flat no feels blunt, soften it as much as you like:

  • “Just this today, thanks!”
  • “I'll stick with what I came for.”
  • “Not right now. I'll come back if I change my mind.”
  • “What's the price without any add-ons?”

Want the extra after all? Get it. The point isn't to never buy anything. It's to decide on purpose, not because someone asked while your card was already out.
